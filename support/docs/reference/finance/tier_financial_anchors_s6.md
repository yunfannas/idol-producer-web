# Scenario 6 Tier Financial Anchors (E-S)

## Purpose

This document is a **working game-balance reference** for Scenario 6 (start date: 2025-07-05). It summarizes the current fanbase and finance anchors discussed for group letter tiers E through S.

These numbers are **simulation assumptions**, not audited real-world accounts. Real groups can reach the same letter tier with very different business models. Use the table as a calibration frame, not as a hard formula.

Fan counts below refer to the **group-level mutually exclusive audience pools**:

- `public`: broad group-level recognition / light fandom;
- `otaku`: fans who actively follow the group as an idol group;
- `core`: the deepest repeat / high-spend supporters.

Member-specific personal public recognition is **not included** in these group fanbase anchors.

A key structural rule is that lower tiers have a larger `otaku + core` share, while higher tiers increasingly accumulate a large public layer.

## Main Fanbase Anchors

| Tier | Reality anchor / archetype | Public | Otaku | Core | Total | Approx. structure |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| E | Small part-time underground idol group | 600 | 215 | 85 | 900 | Public still limited; survival depends heavily on direct fans |
| D | Akishibu project-style professional underground group | 1,800 | 540 | 180 | 2,520 | `10:3:1` anchor |
| C | Jams Collection, S6 start | 6,000 | 1,800 | 500 | 8,300 | Larger scene reach, growing public layer |
| B | NMB48, 2025 major-side archetype | 25,000 | 6,000 | 1,500 | 32,500 | Large organized fandom plus mainstream reach |
| A | =LOVE, 2025 | 80,000 | 16,000 | 4,000 | 100,000 | Arena/IP-scale audience |
| S | Nogizaka46, 2025 | 300,000 | 40,000 | 10,000 | 350,000 | National phenomenon / mass public layer |

### Interpretation

- **E -> D** is primarily the transition from a low-fixed-cost part-time operation to a sustainable professional underground organization.
- **D -> C** is where revenue begins to diversify beyond frequent benefit sessions.
- **C -> B** is the transition into a large, formally organized major / upper-live-idol business.
- **B -> A** adds arena-scale touring and large release/IP economics.
- **A -> S** adds genuine national cultural reach and a very large public layer.

Tier is an organizational / market-scale classification, **not a profit ranking**. A lean C-tier group may have a higher margin percentage than a large B-tier organization.

## Monthly Financial Comparison

The table below uses a common game-design interpretation:

- `monthly operating contribution` = revenue channels after their most direct variable costs (for example benefit materials/member back, CD manufacturing/distribution assumptions, venue/live direct production costs, goods manufacturing);
- `monthly operating cost` = member guarantees, permanent staff, training/admin, recurring production investment, office/organization burden and other recurring overhead;
- `monthly operating surplus` = rough sustainable operating surplus before taxes, debt service and exceptional capital projects.

| Tier | Typical business model | Monthly operating contribution | Monthly operating cost | Rough monthly operating surplus |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| E | Benefit-session-dependent, part-time members, one core staff | ¥3.0-3.5M | ¥1.8-2.5M | **¥0.5-1.5M** |
| D | Professional underground, frequent live + benefit economy | ¥7.5-9.0M | ¥6.0-7.5M | **¥1-2M** |
| C | Strong underground / quasi-major, diversified direct economy | ¥12.5-14.5M | ¥7.5-10M | **¥4-6M** |
| B | Large major / large organized idol operation | ¥30-40M | ¥22-32M | **¥6-12M** |
| A | Arena + release-event + FC/merch/IP model | ¥65-85M | ¥35-55M | **¥25-40M** |
| S | Stadium/arena + mass release + major IP/commercial economy | ¥230-320M | ¥150-220M | **¥60-100M** |

These are intentionally broad ranges. The same tier can contain very different archetypes.

## E-Tier Reference

### Operating shape

A typical E-tier group can survive with surprisingly low total fandom because its cost structure is radically different from D and above:

- members may remain students or have outside jobs;
- fixed member salary can be zero;
- members receive a relatively high sales share instead;
- producer plus only one permanent staff member is plausible;
- temporary event staff are paid hourly;
- costumes are reused for long periods;
- few songs/MVs are produced;
- travel is limited.

### Benefit-session example

Working example:

- 7 members;
- 10 benefit opportunities/month;
- 60 seconds per transaction;
- ¥2,000 per transaction;
- 50% average utilization.

Capacity:

`60 transactions/member-hour x 7 x 10 x 50% = 2,100 transactions/month`

Gross benefit-session sales:

`2,100 x ¥2,000 = ¥4.2M/month`

With no base salary and a relatively high member revenue share, this can still support a small organization because permanent overhead is very low.

## D-Tier Reference — Akishibu-style Professional Underground

### Fanbase anchor

`Public 1,800 / Otaku 540 / Core 180 = 2,520 total`

This replaces the earlier 2,800-fan working anchor and should be treated as the default D midpoint.

### Benefit-session economics

A useful D-style calibration is:

- 7-8 members;
- about 11-12 benefit opportunities/month;
- around 40 seconds / ¥2,000 as a realistic throughput example;
- approximately 60% average utilization in a stable period.

For a seven-member group this produces roughly ¥8.5M/month gross benefit-session sales before direct costs.

Important design implication: D-tier survival should not require every lane to sell out. A professional underground group can survive around 50-65% average utilization if its fixed costs and live schedule are sensible.

### Graduation shock

For a group with low box-oshi transfer, a top-member + bottom-member double departure from an eight-member roster can plausibly reduce short-term benefit-session sales by roughly **20-25%**.

The decline should be split into:

1. structural fanbase loss from member-attached fans leaving;
2. temporary spending / attendance-rate decline;
3. later persistent fanbase decay if stagnation continues.

Graduation announcements can also create a temporary pre-graduation demand buff, sometimes pushing a top member from ~0.8 utilization toward 1.0.

## C-Tier Reference — Jams Collection, S6 Start

### Placement

Jams Collection at the S6 start is used as the central C anchor rather than C+ because its touring scale had not yet become consistently Zepp-level.

### Fanbase

`Public 6,000 / Otaku 1,800 / Core 500 = 8,300 total`

This still excludes member-specific personal public recognition.

### Benefit-session example

Working example:

- 7 members;
- 8 benefit opportunities/month;
- 30 seconds / ¥2,000;
- 70% average utilization.

Monthly units:

`120 slots/hour x 70% x 7 x 8 = 4,704 transactions`

Monthly gross:

`4,704 x ¥2,000 = ¥9.408M`

After direct benefit-session costs, a working monthly contribution around **¥7.5M** is reasonable.

### CD model

Working annual release cadence:

- about `1.2 CDs/year`;
- about `20,000 units/release`.

At the game's current `~¥500 group-net per CD` simplification:

`1.2 x 20,000 x ¥500 / 12 ≈ ¥1.0M/month`

### Live / production model

Working C anchor:

- around one major solo live per month on average;
- roughly 2,000 paid attendance for an important C-level live;
- actual blended ticket price should be below a naive ¥5,000 assumption because low-price general tickets are common;
- major-live net contribution around ¥2-3M/month is a useful working value after direct live costs.

Annual production example:

- 8 new songs;
- 2 MVs;
- 4 costume sets.

C-tier production should be large enough to matter financially but still comfortably fundable by a healthy group.

### Additional revenue

By C tier, monthly economics should also include meaningful:

- merchandise;
- fan club;
- group collaborations / endorsements;
- birthday / anniversary activity.

This is the key structural difference from D: **benefit sessions remain important, but they are no longer the only engine keeping the organization alive.**

## B-Tier Reference — NMB48, 2025 Major-Side Archetype

B should support several different business models. NMB48 is useful because it is neither a Jams-style direct-benefit group nor simply a scaled-down =LOVE.

### Fanbase

`Public 25,000 / Otaku 6,000 / Core 1,500 = 32,500 total`

### Business structure

Primary channels:

- large physical-CD / release-event economy;
- concentrated individual handshake / online talk events;
- permanent theater operation;
- hall-scale major concerts;
- FC/mobile/digital content;
- merchandise;
- group and member media/commercial work.

For this archetype, handshake/online-talk events should usually be treated as **fulfillment costs that support CD demand**, not as a second fully independent revenue stream, otherwise the simulation double-counts the same fan purchase.

### CD scale

A working 2025 NMB-style estimate is approximately two singles per year at roughly 170k-180k units each.

Using the generic ¥500/CD simplification would produce roughly ¥14-15M/month contribution, but a release-benefit-heavy model may deserve a lower effective net per unit because event fulfillment is expensive.

### Theater

The permanent theater should be modeled as both:

- modest recurring ticket contribution;
- core retention / member development / fan-conversion infrastructure.

It does not need to maximize per-show profit.

### Tier meaning

B is where total organization size can become much larger than C without necessarily producing proportionally larger margins. NMB illustrates high revenue plus high organizational burden.

Other valid B archetypes include:

- iLiFE!-style high-frequency live + extremely strong direct benefit monetization;
- ≠ME-style release-event + hall/arena touring.

## A-Tier Reference — =LOVE, 2025

### Fanbase

`Public 80,000 / Otaku 16,000 / Core 4,000 = 100,000 total`

### Business structure

=LOVE is used as the A anchor because it has moved well beyond the routine underground live/benefit model.

Primary channels:

- two major physical releases per year;
- large release-linked online / offline fan events;
- arena touring;
- high-price premium ticket inventory;
- strong FC and merchandise;
- commercial / IP / licensing income;
- member individual work.

Routine taiban participation and post-live tokutenkai are not required for the model.

### CD scale

A working 2025 reference is roughly two singles with combined annual unit volume around 580k+.

At ¥500 group-net per unit:

`580,000 x ¥500 / 12 ≈ ¥24M/month`

### Live scale

Arena tours make live economics a major standalone pillar. Gross ticket sales can exceed ¥100M for a large arena date, but production costs are also very high.

For game balancing, a conservative monthly live contribution around **¥20-30M** is preferable to assuming most arena ticket gross becomes profit.

### Tier meaning

A is the transition into an arena-scale entertainment IP. Direct fandom remains essential, but revenue is spread across releases, arenas, FC, merchandise, content and commercial work.

## S-Tier Reference — Nogizaka46, 2025

### Fanbase

`Public 300,000 / Otaku 40,000 / Core 10,000 = 350,000 total`

This large public share is intentional. S should represent genuine national phenomenon scale rather than simply a very successful idol group.

### Business structure

Primary channels:

- very large physical releases;
- national arena/stadium touring;
- large-scale paid streaming / video products;
- high-volume merchandise;
- FC/mobile ecosystem;
- major advertising / sponsorship / licensing;
- extensive member individual work;
- large media footprint.

### CD scale

A working 2025 reference is roughly 1.7-1.8M physical units across the year's releases.

At ¥500 group-net per unit:

`~1.75M x ¥500 / 12 ≈ ¥73M/month`

### Live scale

Summer national touring plus very large Tokyo dates pushes ticket GMV into a completely different order of magnitude from A and below.

However, S-tier live production is also extremely expensive: stadium/arena rental, large stage systems, lighting, video, security, transport, rehearsal, technical staff and project labor all scale sharply.

For balancing, a monthly live contribution around **¥90-120M** is a more useful reference than raw ticket gross.

### Tier meaning

S is a national entertainment IP with a very large public layer. It should remain rare in the simulation.

## Design Rules Derived From These Anchors

1. **Fanbase is a slow variable; spending/attendance heat is a fast variable.** A group can suffer a rapid revenue decline before the fan count collapses.
2. **Natural monthly inflow and churn should usually remain within roughly 5% of fanbase.** Long periods of slightly negative net movement can still become fatal over six to twelve months.
3. **Graduation should remove member-specific fan equity.** Replacing headcount does not instantly restore benefit-session demand.
4. **Low-tier economics depend on direct access.** E/D groups can be healthy with modest total fan counts if benefit-session utilization is strong and costs are controlled.
5. **C is the diversification threshold.** A healthy C group should be able to fund songs, costumes, MVs and larger solo lives from several revenue channels.
6. **B is not one business model.** NMB, iLiFE! and ≠ME can all be B with very different monetization structures.
7. **A/S should not use the underground post-live tokutenkai model as their primary direct-fan engine.** Release-linked events, FC, arena/stadium touring and IP economics dominate.
8. **Do not rank tiers by profit margin.** Larger organizations can generate much more revenue while carrying proportionally larger member/staff/production burdens.
9. **Personal public recognition is separate from group fanbase.** Individual TV/model/acting success should not automatically inflate the group's public pool one-for-one.
10. **Tier movement should require structural scale change, not a single successful event.** Venue reach, sustainable fanbase, recurring revenue capacity and organizational capability should all matter.
