# Current Japanese Female Idol Live — Rolling 52-Week Research Pass (2026-09)

Working research pass for current-market calibration. Evaluation date: 2026-09-09. Approximate rolling window: 2025-09-10 through 2026-09-09. This is **not** Scenario 6 data.

## Method

Primary Live signal is now **rolling 52-week attributable ticket gross**, not only representative-show size.

For each group record:

- attributable paid own-show count in the window
- annual ticket gross estimate / range
- annual paid admissions estimate when possible
- median / representative show gross
- largest repeatable show gross
- one-off peak gross separately
- ticket structure and confidence

Count at 100%: one-man, official group tour, anniversary/birthday/graduation shows billed as the group's own live.

Joint two-man / co-bill: count only an attributable share when evidence exists.

Do not allocate ordinary taiban/festival/TIF gate receipts to the group. Free-live ticket gross is zero. Benefit-event / cheki revenue is excluded from Live.

The annual-gross estimate below is intentionally shown as a **range** because exact ticket-class sell-through is often unpublished. Venue name alone never determines gross.

## Important interpretations

### 1. Annual gross changes the NANIMONO reading

NANIMONO is the clearest frequency case. Two completed tours alone contribute **29 own paid shows** in the 52-week window. The 2025 tour had 17 shows with S ¥5,000 / A ¥3,000 / female ¥1,500 / rear ¥1,000. The 2026 tour added 12 shows and often cut the rear ticket to ¥500, while large finals used substantial premium tiers.

Therefore NANIMONO can have a respectable annual Live business even while the median show remains only D+ in ticket-gross strength. This is exactly why annual gross and per-show draw must be stored separately.

### 2. Appare! is the opposite of venue-based inference

The 2026 10th-anniversary tour is 15 dates, but the ordinary ticket is explicitly **¥0** and the front-priority ticket is ¥5,000. A large attendance total therefore cannot be converted directly into Live ticket business. The Yoyogi final adds a much more monetized structure, but it should not erase the zero-price nature of the rest of the tour.

### 3. 高嶺のなでしこ vs きゅるして

高嶺のなでしこ has healthier general pricing (~¥7,300) and strong peak events, but the 2026 tour appears not to have filled every venue and current audience reports suggest many stations around the ~2k level.

きゅるして's 2026 hall route is broader: listed dates include Nagoya, Sapporo, Kobe, Niigata, Hiroshima, Sendai, Takamatsu, Kanazawa, Fukuoka, plus two Makuhari Event Hall additions. General tickets are ¥6,600, goods tickets ¥12,500, students ¥3,900. This creates a higher likely 52-week own-live gross even if their ordinary per-show audience is not dramatically larger.

### 4. Jams / INUWASI benefit from frequency

Both are understated by a single flagship-show model. Member birthday one-mans and repeated own tours generate real ticket business and should count. Exact archive reconstruction is still required before locking annual-gross bands.

### 5. yosugala is currently low-frequency/high-ceiling

As of the 2026-09-09 cutoff, its new 15-city 2026 tour has not started. The 52-week result is therefore dominated by completed large one-mans such as EX Theater and NHK Hall. A post-tour evaluation later in 2026 will look very different.

## Next data pass

For final calibration, reconstruct exact or bounded ticket gross for each group by event:

1. recover ticket-class allocation or at least sold-out / remaining status
2. estimate paid admission by class
3. sum attributable gross across the 52-week window
4. record annual paid admissions separately
5. compute median show gross and P75 show gross
6. mark anniversary/birthday/graduation separately but still include actual ticket revenue in annual gross
7. keep one-off peak as a separate field rather than deleting it

Do not define final annual-gross tier bands until at least 15–20 groups have comparable 52-week totals.
