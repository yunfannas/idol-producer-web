Overview

Idol Producer is a management simulation inspired by Football Manager’s information-dense UI. You play as a producer (制作人) responsible for one managed group in a fixed historical scenario. The world is built from a real-world-inspired catalog of groups, idols, songs, and discography data.

Your main pressures are:

  • Time — the calendar advances one day at a time.
  • Cash — lives, training, staff, and scouting cost money.
  • Roster health — members have condition, morale, and training load.
  • Visibility — letter tier and fan reach shape what venues and live types are realistic.

Quick start

  1. Open the game (index.html) and wait for Database ready.
  2. Choose New Game, enter your producer name, and pick a group from the ranked list.
  3. Press Start scenario — the opening date is set by the scenario (e.g. 2025-07-20 for Scenario 6).
  4. Use the left sidebar to open Inbox, Schedule, Lives, Idols, etc.
  5. When ready, click NEXT DAY in the top bar to simulate the next calendar day.
Browse mode — From the opening screen, choose Browse to explore the database without a save. You can view groups, idols, and songs, but NEXT DAY and finances are disabled until you start or load a game.
Language — The in-game UI supports English and Simplified Chinese via the language control on the opening screen. See also the 简体中文手册.

Formed date policy

When catalog data lists a group’s formed date, the game prefers the earliest of: first public announcement, unveiling/reveal, or debut live — whichever is documented first.

Interface

The layout follows a classic management-game pattern:

  • Top bar — Game title, managed group name (clickable), current date, cash (¥), NEXT DAY, and Home menu (save/load/slot).
  • Left sidebar — Section navigation and idol shortlist.
  • Main panel — Content for the active section (tables, detail pages, forms).
  • Status bar — Current view name and turn number.

History buttons (‹ ›) next to Home step back/forward through in-session view history (like browser back within the app).

Core loop

  1. Read the Inbox — Resolve confirmations, live results, and scenario events.
  2. Plan — Check Schedule, Training, Finances, and roster status.
  3. Act — Adjust training, review songs, scout, or book and edit lives in Lives.
  4. Advance — Press NEXT DAY to run the daily simulation.

Each advance increments the turn counter and moves current_date forward. Autopilot systems may book routine lives, apply training effects, and post financial entries depending on save state and tier templates.

Some inbox items block NEXT DAY until you confirm a choice — especially critical scenario events that need your decision.

Letter tiers (S–F)

Every group has a letter tier from S (largest commercial footprint) down to F (livehouse-scale). Tiers drive:

  • Starting cash and monthly cost templates
  • Typical live frequency (taiban vs one-man vs festival)
  • Fan reach floors used in simulation
  • Startup picker ordering (with fans and popularity)

Tiers S–C for ranked groups come from the curated Idol Ranking 2025 mapping. Other groups may be inferred into D–F only. Letter tier is not the same as live type S (special birthday/anniversary overlay).

Booking lives manually

Open Lives and use the workspace tabs:

ScheduledList all upcoming booked lives; click a row to open it in Live.

FestivalFestival timetable appearances for your managed group (e.g. TIF slots imported into the schedule).

New Live workflow:

  1. Pick a type (Routine, Concert, Taiban, or Festival) — presets adjust default duration and tokutenkai timing.
  2. Choose date, venue, start/end, and optional rehearsal times.
  3. Build the setlist: add songs from your group catalog, insert MC or break segments, drag to reorder.
  4. Optionally enable tokutenkai / cheki (talk slots after the show) and a goods booth with expected revenue.
  5. Click Schedule Live — the event appears on Schedule and in Scheduled.

On Schedule, the month grid shows days with booked lives (marked on the calendar). The upcoming week strip starts from your next simulation day. Use NEXT DAY to play out lives on the current date; results land in the inbox and under Past.

After-live meet (tokutenkai)

Types 4–7 often include a tokutenkai (特典会): fans buy goods or tickets for timed talk slots and cheki photos. Default design uses ~60 minutes post-live, mixed ¥2,000 / ¥3,000 talk tiers.

Finances

Cash on hand (¥) appears in the top bar during play. Starting budget depends on scenario number and your group’s letter tier. Ongoing costs include:

  • Member compensation (tier-scaled)
  • Monthly admin and training budgets
  • Live production and venue costs (by type and venue rank)
  • Scout company service fees when engaged

Revenue comes from ticket sales, goods, and event-specific rules. Open Finances for ledgers and monthly breakdowns when available in your build.

Idols & training

Each idol has visible and hidden attributes (0–20 scale), birthday, condition, morale, and group history segments (join/graduate dates per group).

Training

Set per-idol training intensity for the week (vocal, dance, variety, etc.). Training consumes condition and affects morale; overloading weak members risks burnout. Training blocks may schedule morning/afternoon sessions up to a weekly load cap.

Group detail fields

  • Formed — earliest announce/reveal/debut date
  • Producers — credited producer(s), e.g. “former Maneki Kecak member, Fukase Mio”
  • Agencies — management companies when cataloged
  • Discography — releases with track lists linked to the song catalog

Scout

Scout companies (by city and level) search for talent under three channels:

  • Freelancers — idols with no current group
  • Transfers — idols active elsewhere but plausibly recruitable
  • Auditions — open applications from indie hopefuls

Companies charge a service fee and favor idols whose birthplace matches their region (Tokyo, Osaka, Nagoya, etc.). Higher-level firms cover wider nets.

Eligibility rules

  • Idols with a current group are not freelancers.
  • Retired (excluded from scout): if years since last group ended plus current age ≥ 30, they are treated as retired from scouting.
  • A curated solo performer list may be added later for active solo freelancers.

Inbox

The inbox is your operational feed: scheduled live reminders, performance summaries, financial notes, and scenario narrative events. Some messages include choices; pick an option before advancing the day if the UI blocks NEXT DAY.

Future-dated scenario events (debuts, graduations, formation milestones) are applied when their effective date arrives, using the scenario runtime engine.

Saves & browse

Save slots

Games are stored in the browser (local storage) across numbered slots plus an autosave slot. Use Home → Save game / Load game or the opening Load screen. Saves use schema version 11 and embed a snapshot of scenario database rows at new-game time.

What is saved

  • Producer name, managed group, current date, turn, finances
  • Per-idol simulation state (training, condition, morale)
  • Live schedule, inbox, scout engagements, and scenario flags

Browse database

Browse mode loads scenario JSON without creating a save — ideal for researching groups and songs before committing to a career. No cash, no day advance, no shortlist persistence.

Tips

  • Clear the inbox before spamming NEXT DAY — blocked events are easy to miss.
  • Book extra lives in Lives → New Live before big weeks; check Schedule so nothing clashes with auto-booked dates.
  • Lower-tier groups survive on taiban volume; don’t expect stadium economics on tier D–F.
  • Watch training load on members with low condition before big live weeks.
  • Use Groups → wiki links for real-world context on unfamiliar names.
  • Recommended startup groups in Scenario 6 are a curated subset, not the full 400+ group database.
